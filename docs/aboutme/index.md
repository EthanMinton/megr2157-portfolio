*Ethan Minton - Mechanical Engineering / Aerospace Concentration / UNC Charlotte*

As a second-year Mechanical Engineering student at UNC Charlotte, I am driven by the rigorous challenges of designing and building complex physical systems. My passion for the discipline stems from an early interest in mastering math and science, specifically identifying the underlying patterns and details that govern larger physical phenomena people miss every day. A major turning point occurred during my first exposure to calculus and the broader scope of engineering. I pondered the scale and reach of engineering in daily life, from everyday transport to complex computer hardware; it motivated me to move beyond theory into practical application.

My experience is well established within the UNC Charlotte Mechanical Engineering curriculum. I have an in-depth understanding of and the skills to use CAD software such as Creo and Autodesk Fusion, alongside programming languages such as Python and MATLAB. During my freshman year, I was placed within a design group to produce a solar assembly capable of reorienting itself based on light intensity. The project heavily integrated CAD modeling and programming, showcasing our ability not only to understand and use the software, but to apply it to something real. It confirmed that these skills do not just operate within the course load, but can be directly applied to actual engineering design.

By enrolling in the Aerospace Concentration here at UNC Charlotte, I chose to be proactive with the next step of my ambitions. My objective is not just to hone my skills as a mechanical engineering student at Charlotte, but to maximize my abilities to the fullest by mastering the principles of aerodynamics and aeronautical structural design on top of the traditional skills and experiences a mechanical engineer holds. As a developing engineer, who strives for the rigor and work it takes to succeed. I take pride not only in success, but in the struggles that come with it.

**Defending Engineering Decisions**

**08/24/26** - Defending an Engineering Decision is the professional ability to be able to document and research a multitude of solutions. Rigorously determining how different constraints, such as the incredibly complex calculations behind safety, finances, regulations, and time constraints, support and drag different ideas for a solution. Being able to take all of those details and come to a logical conclusion that would be the best path taken. To defend it with all the information you have available while simultaneously observing new information that could change the logic. To admit that I currently know how to do it would be inarguable. Sophomore Design is the first major design course to be foundational to my Engineering experience. There are plenty of bridges that need to be built to not only gain the knowledge but also the skills of an engineer defending their decisions.
